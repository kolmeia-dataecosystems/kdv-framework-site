# Getting started

Choose one use case, one owner and a first working agreement.

## Purpose

Begin with a decision people need to make, not a tool inventory. Identify the intended consumer, the value to observe and the domain that can take responsibility. Keep the first scope small enough to review with its owner and steward.

## Working sequence

1. Name the decision, consumer and intended purpose.
2. Identify Hive, Cell and accountable product owner.
3. Inventory the authorised sources and classify the data.
4. Draft a contract and agree quality thresholds and review authority.
5. Deliver a limited version with the appropriate access controls.
6. Observe use, review limitations and record the next improvement.

## Minimum baseline

Every shared product needs ownership, a contract, a classification, policy-based access and an audit trail. Each ecosystem adapts role names, approval routes, thresholds and review cadences around that minimum.

## Synthetic example

A fictional service-planning team needs a weekly aggregate of requests. The service domain owns the meaning; a subdomain team prepares the data; a product owner agrees freshness and allowed use. The first release is an internal aggregate, not a copy of identifiable source records. The team records quality results and reviews whether the product supports the intended planning decision.
